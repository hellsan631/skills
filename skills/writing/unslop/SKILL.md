# Unslop

The reflex, not the audit. Everything here is unconditional, so it can be applied while
writing without stopping to check anything. Run the `humanize` skill for the
audit: a checker over a file, plus a judgment pass, which is the only way to reach a bar
that rereading never reaches.

Escalate to humanize when the text lands in a file, when the user will publish or share
it, or when a draft has to be right rather than merely clean.

## What to cut

Seven habits cover every rule the checker treats as wrong every time it appears. The
checker knows 210 phrasings of them; you only need the shapes.

Claiming importance instead of showing it. Nothing is "a game-changer", "a cornerstone",
or "a testament to" anything, and saying so stands in for the detail that would have
demonstrated it (`puffery`, `significance-bloat`, `notability-performance`).

Words that occupy space without adding information: "rich tapestry",
"plays a vital role", "it is important to note", "in the realm of".
Cut the clause and see whether anything was lost
(`filler-phrase`, `ecology-filler`, `gap-filling`).

Openings and endings that arrive on rails. A closing paragraph that restates the piece,
or a section that exists because the template had a slot for challenges
(`conclusion-formula`, `challenges-formula`).

Gesturing at substance instead of naming it: "experts say", "studies show",
"this raises questions". Each promises a fact and delivers the shape where the fact goes
(`vague-attribution`, `superficial-analysis`, `abstract-metaphor`).

Talking about the answer instead of giving it: "great question", "I hope this helps",
"as an AI", "it is worth noting that", and the disclaimers nobody asked for
(`sycophancy`, `meta-commentary`, `assistant-artifact`, `didactic-disclaimer`).

The "not just X, but Y" construction, which performs a distinction rather than making
one (`negative-parallelism`).

Leftovers from the machinery: stray citation brackets, unfilled placeholders, a subject
line in something that is not an email (`citation-artifact`, `placeholder-text`,
`subject-line`).

## Put something back

Removing tells is half of it. Prose with every tell removed and nothing in its place
reads as machine-written just as loudly, and that failure has no checker.

Take a position where the medium has an author, including what you would regret about
the option you did not pick. Vary sentence length deliberately, since one short sentence
after two long ones does more than any word choice. Name the awkward part rather than
smoothing it: "fast, but it silently drops duplicates" beats "fast".

Where the writing is reference material, specificity replaces opinion. The named source
or the exact number is what makes a sentence belong to this subject and no other.

## What this deliberately leaves out

Passive voice, bold density, sentence length, triples, and list density are the rules
that fire most often in practice, and none of them are here. Each is legitimate often
enough that a reflex would do more harm than good, so they belong to the checker, which
can count them and report a rate rather than guess at a single instance.

Do not invent facts to replace what you cut. When a sentence turns out to have nothing
behind it, delete it or ask, because a plausible invented detail is a worse failure than
the puffery it replaced.
