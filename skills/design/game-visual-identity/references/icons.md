# Icon identity

Use this guidance for ability icons, items, emblems, and symbol families. Begin with what the player must recognize and the size at which they must recognize it.

## Choose a visual idea that carries the meaning

Give each icon a dominant motif or action. Use the actual mechanic or item identity to choose it. Related concepts need different visible ideas when the player must distinguish them, such as a held shield for guarding and a broken barrier for breaching.

Match complexity to use. A small HUD symbol may need one contour and a gap. A large collectible icon may support a painted scene. Do not shrink a detailed scene and assume its central idea survives.

Separate the subject from its frame and state markers. Faction, rarity, equipped state, cooldown, and selection can have different meanings. Preserve the project's existing assignments. Avoid decorative marks that resemble meaningful counts or status indicators.

## Establish family rules

Inspect neighboring icons and translate the agreed local pillars into family rules. Choose the dimensions of consistency that matter: viewpoint, occupied area, padding, stroke or edge treatment, lighting, palette roles, background treatment, and frame construction. Judge apparent weight as well as matching bounding boxes; a thin staff and a solid shield occupy space differently.

Keep a few of these choices stable while varying subject silhouette and internal structure. A family needs both shared treatment and quick discrimination. Avoid giving every ability the same central orb or burst unless that repetition is meaningful and leaves enough room for distinct identities.

Use an accepted family member as a reference when extending the set. Preserve an existing vector format when vector output is required. Painted raster art can remain painterly; vector rules should not erase the intended medium.

## Test in the intended context

Review icons together at actual display size, including the smallest required size. If the size is unknown during exploration, use clearly labeled provisional previews and leave export dimensions undecided.

Place them on the relevant UI background. Check the dominant shape, internal gaps, edge separation, and collisions with frames or overlays. Reduce unnecessary contrast and detail when downsampling turns the image into noise.

Compare the most easily confused pair. Check whether silhouette, value, or a distinct motif still separates them when hue is unavailable. If meaning depends on color, add another cue and preserve any existing labels or state indicators.

Inspect states that are part of the request, such as disabled or selected. Do not add unsolicited state variants to a simple art exploration.

## Deliver usable assets

A labeled contact sheet helps compare a family. Production delivery also needs the requested individual files with consistent framing and padding. Keep comparison labels out of the runtime art unless text belongs to the icon itself.

Check transparency on the intended backgrounds when alpha is requested. A dark matte or pale halo can remain hidden in a white preview. Confirm actual dimensions and format instead of inferring them from a generation prompt.

Record each icon's meaning and the family rules that produced it. Keep game-specific subjects, palettes, and file specifications in the project rather than adding them to this reusable skill.
