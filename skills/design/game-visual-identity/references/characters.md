# Character identity

Use this guidance for characters, creatures, portraits, and character families. Scale it to the requested deliverable. A portrait does not automatically require a turnaround or animation sheet.

## Make identity survive variation

Identify the features that should survive changes in pose, expression, outfit, and lighting. Useful candidates include body proportions, face structure, hair mass, head shape, a costume silhouette, a signature object, and the placement of color masses. Keep the set small enough to recognize and compare.

Connect appearance to the intended role and personality through visible choices. Weight distribution, posture, equipment, and wear can communicate how a character acts. Avoid inventing biography or changing game mechanics merely to justify a design choice.

Separate anatomy from clothing and equipment. A coat or weapon that creates the silhouette in one pose may disappear in another. Identify what still makes the character recognizable in the actual camera view or portrait crop.

## Compare characters as a cast

Use comparable scale, camera angle, and finish when reviewing a lineup. Distinguish characters through body mass, contour, gesture, value distribution, or signature equipment. Hair color and facial details may disappear at game size.

Give members of a faction or class shared cues without making every subject use the same silhouette. Check similar subjects next to one another. A strong isolated portrait can still fail if it is interchangeable with its neighbors.

For a new direction, a silhouette pass or rough value study may resolve identity before detailed rendering. For an established character, preserve the accepted features and concentrate the change where requested.

## Use sheets to answer a question

Choose views that expose the unresolved issue. Front and side views can clarify proportions; a gameplay view can reveal whether an accessory is visible; expressions can test whether the face remains recognizable. Do not generate extra views as a ritual.

Inspect each generated view for drift in proportions, facial features, costume construction, markings, and equipment. Multiple views on one sheet can contradict one another. Treat a turnaround as a concept reference until its views have been checked for construction consistency.

When a portrait and a small in-game representation share an identity, translate a few defining features into both. Simplification may require exaggerating those features rather than shrinking all the portrait detail.

## Review the result

Check the requested crop and game camera before judging close-up beauty. Look for a clear primary mass, a readable gesture, a recognizable feature, and quiet areas around the focal point. Use silhouette or reduced-size previews when they help test those claims.

Record the defining features that the output actually preserves. If a generated variant changes one, correct it or identify it as a proposed redesign. Keep expression, pose, and rendering changes separate from changes to who the character is.
