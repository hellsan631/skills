# Evaluate the designs this skill produces

Evaluate rendered screens. Written answers can establish that an instruction was understood, but they cannot establish composition, art direction, or visual finish. A skill package passing its checks says nothing about the quality of the screen it produces.

## Controlled comparison

Run the baseline and revised skill separately with the same brief, assets, model, design tools, and time budget. Each run must use its assigned skill. Save the resulting images or prototypes before scoring them. Label which version produced each result after the initial critique when practical, so the reviewer can judge the work before knowing the version.

Start with a HUD and a substantial menu screen because they require different attention and composition decisions. Repeat cases before claiming a reliable improvement. Keep the outputs and reviewer findings together; an independently authored demonstration cannot establish the skill's effect.

## Action HUD brief

Design a 16:9 HUD for a tense third-person monster-hunting game. The player must keep the creature's attack readable while tracking health, stamina, two tools, and a wounded-part objective. Use the supplied busy combat scene. The hunter relies on worn field equipment and hand-marked observations. Deliver a normal-play frame and an urgent low-stamina frame at the same size.

Provide the same scene, icons, fonts, and permissible asset-generation tools to both runs. Judge whether the scene remains dominant, attention changes with urgency, resources remain distinguishable, and the treatment belongs to this hunter's world. Penalize frames that look clear only because the gameplay background was darkened into irrelevance.

## Planning screen brief

Design a 16:9 preparation screen for a squad tactics game. The player chooses a specialist, equips two tools, and spends a shared allowance on upgrades before a mission. The selected specialist's role, current build, candidate upgrade, cost, and remaining allowance must be legible. Use the supplied character art and upgrade relationships. Deliver a selected-build frame and an upgrade-preview frame.

Keep content, assets, and mechanics identical across runs. Judge how the composition conveys the specialist and build, supports the current choice, and shows the proposed change. Reward a visible connection between investment and capability. A collection of equally prominent cards needs a reason rooted in the choice structure.

## Results screen brief

Design a 16:9 results screen for an exuberant arcade racing game. Show the car, finishing place, personal-best improvement, one earned upgrade, and race-again action. The player has just won a difficult race. Use the supplied car render and emblem set. Deliver the payoff frame and the settled summary, plus a brief note describing the transition.

Judge whether the win feels consequential, the accomplishment owns the composition, and the earned upgrade has a clear relationship to the next race. Typography, scale, imagery, and hierarchy must carry the feeling in the frames. Motion quality remains unassessed unless a playable or recorded transition is supplied.

## Design critique rubric

Score only what the artifact demonstrates. Use 0 for absent or contradictory, 1 for generic or weakly resolved, 2 for clear and specific, and 3 for a coherent composition with deliberate emphasis. Attach an observation from the image to each score.

| Dimension | What the reviewer examines |
| --- | --- |
| Gameplay expression | The screen shows the relationships behind the player's choice and the consequence of changing it |
| Focal composition | Scale, visual mass, contrast, and quiet space establish a purposeful reading order |
| Fantasy and identity | Imagery, geometry, type, and materials reinforce the specific role and game moment |
| Information design | Density, grouping, labels, and diagrams support the task without unnecessary explanatory prose |
| Graphic craft | Type hierarchy, alignment, spacing, icon treatment, edges, and asset quality work together at the target size |
| Feedback and emotion | The authored states give selection, commitment, or accomplishment the intended emphasis |

Keep each dimension visible instead of hiding weak visual craft in an aggregate score. Record usability problems that undermine the design, such as unreadable labels or an ambiguous next action. Report taste differences as judgments with reasons. Static images cannot establish responsiveness, physical-device readability, or player comprehension during play.

## Report what was learned

Show the compared artifacts, brief, available assets, model and tools, and reviewer observations. Separate observed improvement in these samples from expectations about future work. Without rendered outputs, the skill's design effectiveness remains untested.
